— 01 / Diagnosis

The hidden tax on engineering velocity.

Every additional CI vendor, code review tool, and bespoke release script compounds into invisible friction. By the time a Fortune 500 platform team notices, it has become a budget line.

When a platform engineering org reaches a thousand contributors, the cost of fragmentation stops being a developer-experience irritant and becomes a balance-sheet item. We have audited release pipelines at insurers, automakers, and federal contractors that had accumulated between fourteen and twenty-six distinct continuous integration vendors, each with its own pricing model, its own permissions surface, and its own theory of what a build artefact is.

The cost of that fragmentation rarely appears as a single number. It hides inside flake retries that cost three engineer-hours each. It hides inside the merge conflicts that compound while a pull request waits for a reviewer. It hides inside the eleventh hot-patch deploy on a Friday afternoon because the previous release went out without a deterministic queue gating it.

By the time the chief information officer asks for a number, the platform team has to triangulate from twelve different dashboards and answer with a margin of error wide enough to drive a fleet through. We have heard it described, more than once, as the only line item nobody ever defends and nobody ever cuts.

Linecraft exists because that situation is not inevitable. The problem is not a shortage of tools. The problem is the absence of an orchestration layer that treats the merge-to-production pathway as a single system, instruments it accordingly, and gives platform leaders one number they can actually defend in a budget review.

“The line item nobody ever defends, and nobody ever cuts. That is exactly the problem Linecraft was built to retire.”
— 02 / Mechanism

How Linecraft works.

Six coordinated planes sit above your existing infrastructure. We do not ask you to rip and replace; we federate, instrument, and govern.

01 —

Signal federation

Linecraft sits as a federation layer above your existing GitHub Actions, Jenkins, Buildkite, and CircleCI runners. Every job, every test, every deploy event flows into a single canonical event log without requiring teams to migrate off the tooling they already trust.
02 —

Deterministic merge queue

A policy-driven merge queue replaces the race-to-merge that creates broken main branches at scale. Queue rules respect change-risk classification, ownership, and deploy windows, and the queue itself is auditable end-to-end for compliance review.
03 —

Flake intelligence

Tests that flake more than a configurable threshold are quarantined automatically, surfaced to the responsible team with a triage workflow, and prevented from blocking unrelated pull requests. Quarantine outcomes are tracked as a first-class metric, not a footnote.
04 —

Policy as configuration

Branch protection, change-management approvals, SOX-relevant separation-of-duties, and HIPAA review trails are defined as configuration that lives in your repository alongside the code. Every change to policy is reviewable, attributable, and reversible.
05 —

Deploy orchestration

A deploy plane coordinates progressive rollouts across regions, fleets, and dependent services. Health gates pause the rollout automatically when a service-level objective trips, and a single rollback command unwinds the entire orchestrated change in seconds.
06 —

Executive instrumentation

One canonical dashboard answers the questions a chief information officer or vice-president of engineering actually asks. PR cycle time, change-failure rate, deploy frequency, and engineer hours recovered are computed consistently and exported to your existing BI stack.

— 04 / Capabilities

Six core capabilities.

Every capability is independently deployable, separately metered, and individually contracted. You can adopt the merge queue alone, or take the platform end-to-end.

№ 01

Merge queue with risk-aware policy

A deterministic queue that respects change-risk classification, ownership, deploy windows, and freeze periods. Every queued change is replayable for audit, every decision is attributable, and queue throughput is itself a first-class metric exposed to your dashboards.

№ 02

Federated runner orchestration

Sits above existing GitHub Actions, Jenkins, Buildkite, CircleCI, and custom runners. We route jobs by cost, capacity, and locality, surface a unified billing view, and give platform teams a single configuration surface to govern across vendors.

№ 03

Flake quarantine and intelligence

Automatic flake detection driven by statistical baseline, with quarantine workflows owned by responsible teams. Flake reports include ownership, recent failure history, and an estimated engineer-hour cost so triage prioritisation becomes a budget conversation.

№ 04

Policy and compliance plane

SOX-relevant separation of duties, HIPAA review trails, SOC 2 evidence streams, and FedRAMP-aligned controls expressed as configuration alongside your code. Every policy change is reviewable, attributable, reversible, and exportable to your audit team without screenshots.

№ 05

Progressive deploy orchestration

A deploy plane that coordinates region-by-region, fleet-by-fleet rollouts with health gates wired into your service-level objectives. Rollback is a single declarative action that unwinds the entire orchestrated change inside ninety seconds for the median customer.

№ 06

Executive instrumentation

One canonical dashboard for the metrics a chief information officer actually defends: PR cycle time, change-failure rate, deploy frequency, time to restore, and engineer hours recovered. Export to Looker, Tableau, Mode, or your in-house warehouse with no transformation tax.

— 06 / Reference

Eight questions, answered.

The questions procurement, security, and platform leadership consistently bring to a Linecraft scoping call. Answered without the marketing prose.

01 —

How does Linecraft integrate with existing CI/CD infrastructure?

Linecraft sits as an orchestration layer above your existing GitHub Actions, Jenkins, Buildkite, or CircleCI pipelines. We do not ask teams to rip and replace; instead we federate signals across runners and add deterministic merge queues, flake detection, and policy gates. Most enterprise deployments are live within fourteen days.

02 —

What is the security and compliance posture?

Linecraft maintains SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, and HIPAA-ready controls. We support BYOK encryption, customer-managed cloud regions in US, EU, and APAC, and offer single-tenant deployments for regulated industries. Annual penetration tests and SBOM transparency are provided to every enterprise contract.

03 —

Can Linecraft run inside our private cloud or air-gapped environment?

Yes. We offer a self-managed deployment that runs in any Kubernetes 1.27 or newer environment, including air-gapped clusters behind a hardened perimeter. Customers in defense, finance, and healthcare run Linecraft entirely inside their own VPCs with no outbound telemetry.

04 —

How does pricing work for organisations with thousands of engineers?

Pricing is custom-quoted based on contributor count, deployment topology, and required SLAs. We do not publish per-seat rate cards because every enterprise contract includes white-glove onboarding, named architects, and dedicated infrastructure capacity. Procurement teams typically engage us through a thirty-minute scoping call.

05 —

What does the implementation timeline actually look like?

Week one is environment provisioning and SSO federation. Week two is repository onboarding for two or three pilot teams. By week four most customers have measurable PR cycle time improvements on the pilot cohort. Full organisation rollout typically completes within ninety days, paced to your release calendar.

06 —

How is Linecraft different from a build cache or remote execution vendor?

Caching and remote execution accelerate the build step. Linecraft governs the entire merge-to-production pathway: queue policy, flake quarantine, deploy gating, and rollback orchestration. We complement caching vendors rather than competing with them, and most customers run both in concert.

07 —

What support model is included with the enterprise tier?

Every contract includes a named technical account manager, a shared Slack Connect channel with our staff engineers, twenty-four-hour response on production incidents, and a quarterly architecture review. Premium support adds a four-hour incident response window and on-call platform engineering pairing.

08 —

Can we trial Linecraft before signing a multi-year contract?

Yes. We run a thirty-day structured pilot on one or two repositories of your choice. Success criteria are defined jointly before kickoff. Roughly nine in ten pilots convert to a paid annual contract; the remainder receive a clean offboarding with all data exported in standard formats.
