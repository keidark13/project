Letter from the founder

Why we built a group instead of one restaurant.

By Mara Whitfield
Founder & Managing Partner
Fort Greene, March 2026

The original plan was one restaurant. A small French-leaning room in Fort Greene with a bar I could see from the pass. We opened Linden in 2019, and by year two I understood something I had not expected — the people I most wanted to keep working with did not all want to cook the same food. My sous chef wanted to bake pizza. My morning prep cook wanted to run a coffee bar. My fish butcher had spent eight years at a sushi counter in Tokyo before he came to me. They were not going to spend the next decade making coq au vin.

So we built a group, slowly, around the people. Nona opened in 2021 in Carroll Gardens because Vincent wanted to. Kuro opened in 2023 because Hiro was ready. Perch opened in 2024 because Adaeze had run the morning shift at Linden for three years and had a clearer idea of what a Brooklyn brunch room should be than anyone I knew. Each restaurant exists because a specific person was ready to lead it. That is the only rule we have managed to keep.

Pages 30 – 31 · The case

How we are different from most restaurant groups.

The usual model

Restaurant groups usually —

  • — Open the same restaurant in different zip codes and call it a group.
  • — Centralize purchasing to chase margin, which dulls every kitchen.
  • — Pay back-of-house investors first, line cooks last.
  • — Replace chefs every 18 months when revenue dips a quarter.
  • — Treat the corporate office as the brand, not the rooms.
  • — Expand into a fifth concept before the fourth is stable.
The Common Table

We —

  • + Open a new concept only when a specific chef is ready to lead it.
  • + Buy independently per kitchen — Vincent's tomatoes are not Asha's tomatoes.
  • + Pay tipped staff a guaranteed floor and share service charge transparently.
  • + Promote from within — three of our four head chefs were line cooks at Linden.
  • + Run no corporate office. Mara works the bar at Linden on Tuesdays.
  • + Wait roughly 24 months between openings, sometimes longer.
Pages 32 – 33 · Looking ahead

What we look for in a fifth concept.

We have a short list of neighborhoods and a longer list of chefs. We will not move on either until both are ready. These are the rules we measure against.

No. 01

Neighborhood-first.

The chef has to live in the zip code, or want to. We do not open in neighborhoods our team has only visited.

No. 02

Chef-led, not concept-led.

We do not chase trending cuisines. We back a specific cook with a specific point of view and build the room around them.

No. 03

Sub-$80 per person.

Three of our four concepts sit under $80 per head, tax and tip included. Kuro is the exception; the next will not be.

No. 04

Family-owned suppliers.

At least 70% of food cost goes to family-owned farms, mills, fisheries, and butchers. We audit this twice a year.

No. 05

A no-tipping path.

Every concept offers a service-included pricing option for private events. We are working toward making it the default.

No. 06

Walkable from the last one.

We have no plans to leave Brooklyn. Five boroughs is already a wider radius than most groups can hold together.

Pages 44 – 47 · Common questions

Things guests ask us.

A short list of what comes up most often at the host stand. If your question isn't here, email hello@thecommontable.co and we'll reply within one business day.

01 Are gift cards good across all four restaurants? +
Yes. One gift card, four concepts. Redeemable for food, beverage, private events, and merchandise at Linden Room, Nona Pizza, Kuro Counter, and Perch Coffee & Brunch. Digital cards arrive by email instantly; physical cards mail within three business days. Cards never expire and are reloadable in any amount over $25.
02 Can you accommodate a large party at any of the concepts? +
Linden Room seats parties up to 28 in its back room with a prix-fixe menu. Nona Pizza accommodates groups up to 40 with family-style ordering and shared antipasti. Kuro Counter is fixed at 10 seats — full buy-outs are available Sunday and Monday. Perch hosts brunch groups up to 24 on weekends with 72 hours' notice.
03 Do you host private events? +
Three of our four venues handle private events. Linden Room offers full buy-out for 60 to 90 guests with two service options. Nona Pizza opens for daytime events of 30 to 80 in its enclosed garden. Kuro Counter offers full omakase buy-outs for groups of exactly 10. Email events@thecommontable.co for date holds, menus, and pricing.
04 How do you handle dietary restrictions across the group? +
Every menu at every concept has a vegetarian path, a gluten-free path, and a tree-nut-free path noted at the bottom of the printed menu. For dairy, shellfish, or alpha-gal allergies — and for kosher or halal accommodation — please flag at booking. Our chefs will build a substitute course rather than scratch ingredients off an existing plate.
05 Is there a loyalty program across the four restaurants? +
Yes. The Common Table membership tracks visits across all four concepts. Every tenth visit earns a complimentary tasting course at Kuro Counter, a bottle of natural wine at Linden Room, a whole pie at Nona Pizza, or a one-month coffee subscription at Perch. Sign-up is free at any host stand or at thecommontable.co/membership.
06 What is your cancellation policy? +
Standard reservations at Linden Room, Nona Pizza, and Perch Coffee can be cancelled or modified up to 24 hours before the booking with no fee. Kuro Counter omakase requires 72-hour notice — inside that window, the deposit covers fish that has already been purchased and prepared for your seating. We do not charge no-show fees on first-time guests.
07 Do you offer outdoor seating? +
Linden Room operates a 24-seat sidewalk café from May through October, weather permitting. Nona Pizza has a year-round enclosed back garden with heaters. Perch maintains 12 outdoor seats April through November on its 7th Avenue frontage. Kuro Counter is indoor-only by design — the menu progression depends on a controlled room temperature.
08 Is The Common Table opening additional locations? +
We are looking, slowly. Our criteria are listed under What We Look For in a 5th Concept above — neighborhood-first, chef-led, sub-$80 per person, and walkable from one of our existing rooms. We open a new restaurant roughly every 24 months, only in neighborhoods our team already lives in or near. We have no plans to expand outside the five boroughs of New York.
